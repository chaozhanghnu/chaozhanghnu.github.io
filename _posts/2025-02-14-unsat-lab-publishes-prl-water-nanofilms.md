On **14 February 2025**, researchers from the **UNSAT Lab at Hunan University** published a study titled **“Water Nanofilms Facilitate Ice Crystal Growth across Droplets”** in **Physical Review Letters (PRL)**.

The study revealed a mechanism by which nanometer-thick water films can transmit freezing between separate supercooled droplets. Using high-speed imaging, the researchers showed that a connected water nanofilm thicker than a critical value of approximately **4.5 nm** can facilitate the propagation of a freezing front from one droplet to its neighbors. The propagation rate through the nanofilm was approximately **40%** of the recalescence-front growth rate within a droplet, with both processes described by kinetic crystal growth theory.

The **College of Civil Engineering at Hunan University** was the primary institution for the work. **Shaojie Hu** was the first author and **Prof. Chao Zhang** was the first corresponding author. The study was coauthored by **Ningning Zhao, Fuxiang Li, Renpeng Chen, and Dani Or**, and was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China.
